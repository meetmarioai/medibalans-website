Why SIBO is missed

IBS without an answer
is often SIBO

SIBO produces no specific findings on standard blood tests, no abnormalities on stool testing, and is not visible on gastroscopy or colonoscopy. The only way to demonstrate it is a breath test — measuring the gases bacteria in the small intestine produce when they ferment sugar.

Hydrogen (H₂) is produced primarily by bacteria and is the marker for classic SIBO. Methane (CH₄) is produced by archaea and is the marker for IMO — a related condition causing constipation-predominant IBS. Many laboratories measure only hydrogen and miss the IMO diagnosis entirely. MediBalans always measures both.

Global Constraint Rule · SIBO as a secondary constraint. Within the GCR framework SIBO is almost always secondary to a primary constraint — usually reactive foods identified via ALCAT. Food reactivity drives the intestinal inflammation and barrier dysfunction that create the conditions for overgrowth. Treat SIBO without addressing the primary driver and it returns. The SIBO test confirms the diagnosis and guides treatment; ALCAT identifies the cause.

SIBO vs IMO

Two conditions, one analysis

SIBO — small intestinal bacterial overgrowth

Hydrogen-producing bacteria (H₂). Bacteria ferment sugar in the small intestine and produce hydrogen gas. Hydrogen drives diarrhoea, cramping and bloating — classic diarrhoea-predominant IBS. Measured as the rise in H₂ after substrate intake.

IMO — intestinal methanogen overgrowth

Methane-producing archaea (CH₄). Archaea (Methanobrevibacter smithii) produce methane, which slows intestinal transit. Strongly associated with constipation, bloating and constipation-predominant IBS. Requires specific CH₄ measurement — missed if only H₂ is measured.

How the test is done

Four steps at home

01 Preparation. For two days before the test you follow a specific diet that reduces background fermentation. Antibiotics must not have been taken in the previous four weeks. Detailed instructions are included in the kit.

02 Baseline breath. On the test day, fasted since the evening before, a baseline breath sample is collected into one of the prepared tubes. It matters that this follows the protocol exactly.

03 Substrate and sampling. You drink the lactulose or glucose solution and collect breath samples every 20 minutes for 2–3 hours. Each sample captures gas production in real time as the substrate is fermented.

04 Analysis and interpretation. Samples are sent to Genova's laboratory. Results are reviewed by Dr Mario Anthis, who interprets the pattern of the H₂ and CH₄ curves and designs a specific treatment protocol based on SIBO type and severity.

Frequently asked questions

SIBO testing explained

SIBO (Small Intestinal Bacterial Overgrowth) means bacteria that normally belong in the colon colonise the small intestine in abnormally large numbers. The small intestine is normally relatively sterile — bacterial overgrowth there disrupts digestion, competes for nutrients and produces gases (hydrogen and methane) that directly cause bloating, pain, diarrhoea and/or constipation. SIBO has been identified as a primary mechanism behind up to 80% of IBS cases.

The test is performed at home and requires no blood samples. You drink a lactulose or glucose solution and collect breath samples every 20 minutes over 2–3 hours into prepared tubes included in the kit. The samples are sent to the laboratory for analysis of hydrogen (H₂) and methane (CH₄) — the gases produced by bacteria in the small intestine. The kit is posted to your home with detailed instructions.

Glucose is absorbed rapidly in the upper small intestine and only produces a positive test where SIBO is proximal. Lactulose reaches the whole small intestine and can identify distal SIBO as well — but yields more false positives. MediBalans selects the protocol based on the patient's symptom profile and clinical picture. In most cases the lactulose protocol is used for maximum sensitivity.

IMO (Intestinal Methanogen Overgrowth) is a related but distinct condition in which methane-producing archaea (Methanobrevibacter smithii) overgrow in the gut. Methane slows intestinal transit and is strongly associated with constipation-predominant IBS and chronic constipation. IMO is identified specifically by measuring methane (CH₄) in the breath test — a component that standard SIBO tests sometimes miss.

Within the GCR framework SIBO is usually a secondary consequence rather than the primary cause. Reactive foods identified via ALCAT drive intestinal inflammation and barrier dysfunction, which create the conditions for bacterial overgrowth. The SIBO test confirms presence and type (hydrogen versus methane) and guides the specific antimicrobial treatment. GI Effects® provides the parallel microbiome picture. Treatment always addresses the underlying driver, not merely the overgrowth itself.
